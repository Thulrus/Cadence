Welcome to our annual community garden report. This year was our biggest yet, and I have a lot of numbers to share, so bear with me.

We started the season on March 15 with 42 plots. By the end of May every plot was taken, and we had a waiting list of 17 families. Next year we plan to add 12 more plots on the north side of the lot, which would bring us to 54 in total.

Our volunteers logged 1,250 hours this season. That is up about 30% from last year, when we logged roughly 960 hours. The single busiest day was June 3rd, when 38 people showed up for the spring cleanup and stayed until 4:30 p.m.

The finances are in good shape. We raised $4,800 from plot fees and another $2,350 from the plant sale. Our biggest expense was water, which came to $1,975 for the year. We also spent $640 on new tools and $215 on seeds. After everything, we ended the year with $3,120 in the bank, which is the most we've had since the garden opened in 2009.

The harvest was remarkable. Members reported growing over 2,000 pounds of produce, and we donated 475 pounds to the food pantry on 5th Street. Tomatoes were the big winner again, at about 40% of the total, followed by squash at 15% and beans at 12%.

A few dates to remember for next year. Plot renewals open on January 10 and close on February 28. The first workday is April 5, 2025, starting at 9:00 a.m. The plant sale will be on May 17, and the harvest festival is set for September 20.

Finally, a big thank you to the 3 people who served on the board this year, and to all 86 members who made this possible. Here's to an even bigger season, and maybe, just maybe, 3,000 pounds of tomatoes.
